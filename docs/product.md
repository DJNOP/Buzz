# Product Definition

## Vision

Create an original local multiplayer party game built around short, accessible, competitive minigames. The main experience happens on a shared television or computer screen while two to four players use phone browsers as simple controllers.

The experience should be family-friendly and still entertaining for adults playing with friends. It is not specifically a children's game and not specifically a drinking game. Adults may create their own drinking rules socially, but alcohol must not be part of the core product, branding, scoring system, or initial gameplay.

## Experience principles

- Keep players focused on the shared screen instead of asking them to watch their phones continuously.
- Make minigames understandable within a few seconds.
- Target approximately 30–90 seconds for a normal minigame.
- Favour immediate, social, competitive feedback over complex controls or lengthy instructions.
- Support two to four players initially.
- Use a simple tournament or cup containing several minigames as the initial overall structure.
- Do not include a board-game structure in the MVP.

## Initial controller

The initial phone controller contains:

- one large primary action button; and
- four smaller coloured buttons.

This standard five-button controller is the core control scheme for at least the
first three prototype minigames. The current themed validation slice uses one
large round A above four stacked, full-width controls labelled 1 RED, 2 BLUE, 3
YELLOW, and 4 GREEN. This is a provisional ergonomics and identity choice to
test with real players, not a final controller-design commitment.

Individual minigames may eventually use alternative phone layouts, gestures, hidden information, sliders, directional controls, or other inputs. Those options are deferred until the standard controller has been implemented and tested; they are possibilities, not current requirements.

## MVP access and display

- The first host runs in a desktop browser.
- The host can be displayed on a television using HDMI, casting, or another normal display connection.
- Phone controllers run in browsers, with no application installation required.
- Players should eventually join by scanning a QR code or entering a short room code.
- Initial communication is over the same local network.
- A native mobile-controller application and a dedicated smart-TV or television-platform application may be considered much later, but both are outside the MVP.

## MVP boundaries

The MVP does not include:

- a board-game metagame;
- native phone applications;
- dedicated smart-TV applications;
- user accounts or authentication;
- online matchmaking;
- payments, analytics, or advertising; or
- production cloud infrastructure.

## Originality and intellectual-property boundary

The following requirements are non-negotiable:

- Do not use the names Buzz, Buzz! Junior, Jungle Party, PlayStation, Sony, or other existing game brands inside the product.
- Do not copy existing characters, monkeys, environments, artwork, animations, music, sound effects, dialogue, logos, branding, interface graphics, or controller appearance.
- Do not reproduce the exact visual design of an existing physical controller.
- The general concept of a large action button and four coloured inputs may inspire functional simplicity, but this product's arrangement, shapes, styling, symbols, terminology, feedback, characters, world, and presentation must be original.
- Design concrete minigames independently rather than directly recreating existing minigames.
- Document the source and licence of every temporary external asset.
- AI-generated or AI-assisted artwork and animation may be used for internal concepts and prototypes.
- Before any commercial release, document the generation method, source, licence, intended commercial usage rights, and relevant restrictions of every external or AI-assisted asset.
- Treat final character consistency, animation quality, audio, branding, and commercial rights as a deliberate production pipeline. Do not assume AI output is automatically consistent, production-ready, or commercially cleared.

## Product validation goal

Early prototypes should validate the controller-to-host loop before expanding scope: connection reliability, response latency, five-button ergonomics, accessibility, whether simple minigames work on a shared screen, and whether players genuinely want another round.

## Provisional Event Rescue slice

Signal Sprint currently stages the validation loop as an original event venue
being prepared by four identical temporary service robots. Player identity is
redundant by colour and shape: red/circle, blue/square, yellow/triangle, and
green/diamond. The robots, venue, station tasks, labels, and CSS/SVG styling are
temporary test content. A physical playtest must determine whether this world
improves shared-screen clarity and appeal before any final character, theme,
brand, or art-production decision.
